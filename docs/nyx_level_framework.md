# Technical Ladder — Nyx Semiconductor

Internal reference, compiled from Nyx's HR records as part of acquisition diligence. This
is Nyx's own leveling document, not a translation of it — read literally by the acquired-side
advocate, the same way Meridian's leveling agent reads its own framework literally.

---

## 1. Company profile

Nyx Semiconductor is a fabless silicon company (~104 people, late-stage private) building
the NX-100 through NX-600 product lines — SoCs, high-speed SerDes PHYs, and mixed-signal
subsystems. The company is small enough that most senior engineers still carry individual
technical ownership alongside any people-management responsibility; there has never been a
standalone management track. A small Photonics R&D group, pursuing co-packaged optical I/O
for a future product generation, was stood up recently and sits outside every established
job family — Nyx itself has not yet decided how it fits into the ladder below.

---

## 2. Track structure

One ladder, five levels, individual-contributor in name and in practice:

**MTS I → MTS II → Senior MTS → Principal MTS → Distinguished MTS**

There is no separate manager track. An engineer who takes on people-management
responsibility keeps their MTS level and adds a functional label describing the
responsibility — e.g. **"Senior MTS, Engineering Manager"** or **"Principal MTS, Engineering
Manager."** The MTS level and the functional label are set independently:

- The MTS level answers *how deep and how broad is this person's technical scope*.
- The functional label (when present) answers *do they also run a team*.

A manager's MTS level does not rise automatically with headcount managed, and an individual
contributor is never blocked from Principal or Distinguished MTS for lacking direct reports.
Span of control is real evidence of organizational trust, but it is evidence for the MTS
level like any other — not a separate track with its own ladder.

**Fellow** is not a level. It is an honorific held by a small number of people with
sustained, externally recognized technical impact. A Fellow keeps their MTS level (in
practice always Principal or Distinguished) and "Fellow" is added as a prefix or, in some HR
records, used alone where the rest of the title has been dropped. Fellow has no scope anchor
of its own in this document — it isn't a scope tier, it's Nyx's way of publicly marking a
track record. What it implies about a given person's technical scope has to be read from
their actual work, the same as anyone else's.

---

## 3. Leveling principle

Nyx levels are set primarily by technical depth and breadth: how much of a domain a person
has mastered, and how far their judgment is trusted to run without another engineer
checking it first. Organizational reach — how many teams' work depends on this person's
decisions — matters, but it has always been read here as a *consequence* of technical depth,
not a separate axis to be weighed against it. In practice the two move together at Nyx: a
person who has gone deep enough in one area to become the company's last word on it belongs
at the top of the ladder for that reason alone, whether or not their work has ever crossed
into a second discipline. Breadth across disciplines is common at the top of the ladder
because deep specialists tend to accumulate it along the way, not because it's required to
get there.

Title is not self-executing. A title on an offer letter or an HR record reflects a leveling
decision someone already made — it is a record of the outcome, not the evidence for it.
Re-leveling a role means reading what the person actually did, not re-stating the title.

---

## 4. Level anchors

| Level | Technical depth & autonomy | Typical indicators |
|---|---|---|
| **MTS I** | Works within an established area under a more senior engineer's direction; contributes a defined piece of a larger block rather than owning one outright. | First engineering role, or first 1–2 years at Nyx. One tapeout, one sub-block, supervised sign-off. |
| **MTS II** | Owns a component or sub-block independently, start to finish; makes routine technical judgment calls without asking. | Full, unsupervised ownership of at least one tapeout-scale piece of work, still within a single area. |
| **Senior MTS** | A recognized authority within their immediate discipline; owns a full block or subsystem across multiple tapeouts, and is trusted with calls that used to need a more senior sign-off. | Multi-tapeout ownership of a subsystem; may informally mentor one or two more junior engineers; technical decisions are rarely second-guessed within their own team. |
| **Principal MTS** | An authority the rest of the org defers to on a defined technical area; the work sets a pattern or a methodology that other engineers build on, not just a single component. | Cross-tapeout, cross-team technical ownership — a methodology, an IP block reused across product lines, a subsystem spanning more than one team. May lead a small technical sub-team without that being a requirement of the level. |
| **Distinguished MTS** | Company-wide authority on a technical domain; the person other Principal MTS engineers escalate to. Decisions shape how Nyx builds silicon for years, not for one product cycle. | Domain ownership spanning the product roadmap rather than one product; final technical sign-off authority within the domain; often, but not always, paired with recognition outside Nyx. |

Levels are bands, not thresholds with a single deciding fact — two people at the same level
can look quite different, and a given person's evidence can plausibly read as either of two
adjacent levels depending on which indicator is weighted more heavily. That ambiguity is
real; Nyx has never fully resolved it either.

## 5. Managerial scope

Nyx has around a dozen people carrying a functional management label alongside their MTS
level. The label describes what they run, not how senior they are:

- **"[Level] MTS, Engineering Manager"** — day-to-day management of an engineering team:
  sprint or tapeout planning, performance reviews, hiring, and representing the team in
  cross-functional reviews.
- Budget authority, where it exists, is described separately in the person's record — it is
  not implied by the manager label itself, and plenty of engineering managers at Nyx have
  none beyond headcount requisitions.

Because the MTS level is set by technical scope and the manager label is set by people
scope, the two can combine in ways that look unusual from outside: a Senior MTS engineering
manager with six reports and a Principal MTS individual contributor with none are both
real, current patterns at Nyx, and neither is a mistake in the data.

---

## 6. Fellow

Nyx has granted the Fellow honorific to a handful of people across its history, always for
sustained impact recognized outside the company — patents, conference presentations,
standards-body participation, or an external reputation in a technical community. It has
never been granted for internal reputation alone, however senior. A Fellow's MTS level is
set the same way as anyone else's, from their described scope; "Fellow" says something
about their track record, not about which level that scope lands at.

---

## 7. Job families represented in the current population

- **Digital Design** — RTL Design, Microarchitecture
- **Analog & Mixed-Signal** — Analog Design, RF
- **Photonics** — a standalone R&D group formed within the last two years, pursuing
  co-packaged optical I/O. It does not sit under Digital Design or Analog & Mixed-Signal,
  and Nyx has not yet defined family-specific anchors for it beyond the general ladder above
  — leveling in this group has so far been done by direct comparison to the closest analog
  engineers on the same floor, not against a written standard.

---

## 8. A note on completeness

This document reflects Nyx's HR records as compiled at signing. It was never written for an
acquisition — Nyx built it for its own use, to keep leveling roughly consistent as the
company grew past the point where everyone could just ask the CTO. It does not anticipate,
and makes no attempt to reconcile with, any other company's job architecture.
